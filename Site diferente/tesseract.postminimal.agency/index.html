tesseract

a small universe in four dimensions

about

A small universe you can hold in four dimensions.

It lives on a 3-sphere, the surface of a four-dimensional ball, seen through stereographic projection, the way a map flattens a globe. Its light is arranged in Hopf fibers: every point belongs to exactly one circle, and every circle is linked through every other.

In 1880 Charles Howard Hinton suggested that anything moving through our three dimensions might be a cross-section of something still, in four. He named the fourth direction’s two ways ana and kata, and in 1888 coined the word tesseract.

Because this space is a sphere, a signal sent from any point spreads, converges on the exact opposite point, and comes back. The music is that geometry, played.
